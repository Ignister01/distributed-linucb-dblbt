\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Wi-Fi and 5G New Radio in unlicensed spectrum (NR-U) contend without a common
scheduler. Both technologies defer to detected energy and count down a random
backoff, but the surrounding procedures differ. Wi-Fi follows DCF/EDCA after
an inter-frame deferral. NR-U category-4 LBT must also obey channel-occupancy
limits and align access to an NR transmission boundary
\cite{ieee80211_2020,3gpp37213,naik2020next}. When several counters expire
together, collisions waste airtime and extend delay tails.

DB-LBT replaces part of this repeated random recovery with a counter derived
from local countdown interruptions \cite{kosek2022dblbt}. The resulting
spacing can approach a collision-free access order without node identities or
cross-technology messages. Later work showed that the mechanism also works
under non-full-buffer traffic, random busy slots, sensing perturbation, and
asymmetric energy detection \cite{tinnirello2026deterministic}. Those regimes
also expose a practical weakness: one fixed recovery tuple must serve both a
quiet cell and a cell whose active set or queues have changed.

Consider a node that has just completed an attempt. It knows whether CCA froze
its countdown, how long packets waited, whether ACK/HARQ succeeded, and how its
queue evolved. It does not know every peer queue or the instantaneous channel
gain. The useful control question is which legal DB-LBT recovery profile should
be used next. We formulate DB-LBT parameter adaptation as a protocol-constrained
contextual bandit problem under locally observable MAC information.

This formulation targets fixed indoor cells with mutual sensing and coarse
changes in offered load, active contenders, or persistent occupancy. Within a
phase, local histories must remain stable long enough to guide several updates.
Across phases, the same histories must change enough to distinguish profiles.
Such traffic and occupancy structure is common in scheduled application bursts,
shifted active sets, and periodic incumbent activity even though individual
CCA samples are random.

The contributions are threefold. First, we define a 24-arm contextual bandit
whose actions are legal DB-LBT tuples; learning cannot alter sensing thresholds,
Wi-Fi deferral, NR-U synchronization or occupancy limits, PHY rate, or
retransmission semantics. Second, we derive conditions under which local
CCA/MAC history provides an actionable context: the contention abstraction must
hold, regimes that require different actions must be locally separable, and
their dwell time must exceed observation and adaptation windows. Third, paired
event experiments show that adaptation preserves the stable DB-LBT regime and
provides a small held-out gain, while a packet-level cross-check exposes a
mismatch for future experiments. Fast radio variation and strategic
co-adaptation are treated as scope boundaries in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
