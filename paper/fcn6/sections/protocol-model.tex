\section{Local Information and Protocol Abstraction}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Where Adaptation Enters}

Let $\mathcal K=\mathcal K_{\mathrm W}\cup\mathcal K_{\mathrm N}$ contain
the Wi-Fi APs and NR-U gNBs sharing one channel. Wi-Fi decrements a counter only
after the applicable inter-frame deferral. NR-U category-4 LBT counts idle
sensing slots and must also respect its priority class, maximum channel
occupancy, and transmission-boundary alignment
\cite{kosek2021downlink,3gpp37213}. DB-LBT acts after an ACK/HARQ outcome: it
supplies the next recovery counter before either native access procedure starts.

For a local countdown slot $\ell$, $c_k(\ell)$ evolves as
\begin{equation}
c_k(\ell+1)=
\begin{cases}
c_k(\ell)-1, & C_k(\ell)=0,\\
c_k(\ell), & C_k(\ell)=1,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:countdown}
\end{equation}
where $C_k=1$ denotes a busy CCA sample. A maximal busy episode freezes the
counter and increments the local interruption count $i_k$. In a mutually
detectable collision domain, each successful peer contributes roughly one such
episode per cycle, giving the operational estimate $\hat n_k\approx1+i_k$.

\subsection{From the Channel to Local Context}

The received power from transmitter $j$ includes path loss, shadowing, and
small-scale fading through gain $G_{jk}(t)$. LBT compresses their sum into a
threshold decision, while the receiver compresses SINR $\gamma_k$ into an
ACK/HARQ outcome:
\begin{align}
C_k(t)&=\mathbb 1\!\left\{N_k+\sum_{j\ne k}a_j(t)P_jG_{jk}(t)
\geq\Gamma_k\right\},
\label{eq:cca}\\
\Pr\{Y_k=1\mid\gamma_k,u_k\}
&=1-\mathrm{PER}_{\tau_k}(\gamma_k,u_k).
\label{eq:success}
\end{align}
Both mappings are nonlinear. A small received-power change can flip CCA, and a
failed attempt can reflect overlap, fading, hidden interference, or decoding.
The learner receives protocol outcomes, not the underlying gains.

Three assumptions recover the contention abstraction used by DB-LBT: (A1)
every co-channel transmission exceeds every contender's CCA threshold; (A2)
isolated reception succeeds with probability near one; and (A3) simultaneous
transmissions have no capture. Under A1--A3,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_k(\ell)&=\mathbb 1\!\left\{\sum_{j\ne k}a_j(\ell)>0\right\},\\
Y_k(\ell)&=1\iff\sum_j a_j(\ell)=1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reduction}
\end{equation}
One zero counter then succeeds and multiple zeros collide. This reduction
explains why busy periods and interruptions carry contention information even
though the radio channel itself is random.

The context groups 11 quantities already available at an AP or gNB. Busy
fraction and interruptions summarize $C_k$; success, failure, and
retransmissions summarize $Y_k$; queue occupancy, arrivals, delay, and
effective-data time describe demand and service.

The feature groups respond at different moments in an access cycle. When
several idle queues become active together, the first attempts expose the
activation range through queue growth, delay, and early CCA freezes. Once the
active set settles, repeated interruptions and retransmissions describe how
well the deterministic and random recovery branches maintain spacing. A
periodic external transmitter creates another pattern: busy fraction rises at
regular intervals even when the peer population is unchanged. The controller
does not have to label these causes. It only needs their local traces to rank
the available recovery profiles.

Let $z$ denote a MAC occupancy regime and $\bar x_k(z)$ its mean local
context. Table~\ref{tab:context} shows why each feature group can change when
load, active set, or external occupancy changes. Local information separates
the actions that matter when
\begin{equation}
q^\star(z)\ne q^\star(z')
\ \Longrightarrow\
\|\bar x_k(z)-\bar x_k(z')\|_2\geq\delta_x>0.
\label{eq:identifiability}
\end{equation}

Timing supplies the second condition. Five scales describe the operating
region: $T_{\rm phy}$ for fast radio variation, $T_{\rm ctx}$ for context
collection, $T_{\rm adapt}$ for one learning response, $T_{\rm dwell}$ for a
regime, and $T_{\rm hor}$ for the operating horizon. The intended setting
satisfies
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm phy}\ll T_{\rm ctx}\lesssim T_{\rm adapt}\ll T_{\rm dwell}
<T_{\rm hor}.
\label{eq:timescale}
\end{equation}
Fast randomness can then average into local history before the regime changes,
while at least one change occurs soon enough to make adaptation useful.
Equations~\eqref{eq:identifiability} and \eqref{eq:timescale} give local
observations their precise role: they need only distinguish DB-LBT actions, not
reconstruct the physical channel.
