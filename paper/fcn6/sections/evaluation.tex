\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The evaluation reuses the completed paired-seed data. The event simulator
implements \eqref{eq:reduction} for \num{100000} contention rounds, with
\SI{2}{\milli\second} transmissions, equal Wi-Fi/NR-U contention windows
$[15,63]$, and a \SI{250}{\micro\second} NR-U synchronization grid. Ten formal
seeds are disjoint from the three pretraining seeds. Pairing fixes exogenous
random streams across policies. Held-out conditions cover unseen symmetric and
asymmetric densities, two Poisson rates, two periodic occupancies, sensing
perturbation, and a combined dynamic case. A separate
ns-3.35/5G-LENA/NR-U model provides the packet-level cross-check.

The reporting utility is distinct from the local reward in
\eqref{eq:reward}:
\begin{align}
U_D^{\rm eval}&=1-\operatorname{clip}
 (D_{95}/500\,\mathrm{ms},0,1),\\
U_{\rm eval}&=(A_{\rm total}+U_D^{\rm eval}+J)/3-0.25P_{\rm col}.
\label{eq:evaluation-utility}
\end{align}
It combines effective data airtime, P95 access delay, Jain fairness, and event
collision probability. Full scenario matrices, seeds, and component metrics
appear in the appendix.

The experiments follow the control argument in order. Mechanism validation
checks that online selection does not erase deterministic spacing in a stable
cell. Held-out event scenarios then ask whether the pretrained controller
transfers to unseen density, asymmetry, traffic, and occupancy conditions. The
packet model replaces the ideal contention rule with EDCA, HARQ, PHY
interference, and NR-U alignment. Finally, ablations compare contextual and
context-free selection and remove feature groups from the same dynamic case.
This sequence keeps a scheduling result, a generalization result, and a
packet-level result from being treated as interchangeable evidence.

Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} first asks whether adding a learner disturbs the
underlying schedule. In static $4+4$, deterministic spacing removes almost all

retains the mechanism and improves utility from $0.961087$ to $0.961785$.

\subsection{Generalization beyond Training Conditions}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \subfloat[Mean utility across held-out scenarios.]{%
    \includegraphics[width=.47\textwidth]{results-heldout.pdf}}
  \hfill
  \subfloat[Paired adaptive-minus-fixed-TMC differences.]{%
    \includegraphics[width=.51\textwidth]{results-differences.pdf}}
  \caption{Held-out event evaluation over ten paired seeds. In (a), the axis is
  truncated and values are printed beside the bars. In (b), differences are
  scaled by $10^3$; bars are 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals. Green favors
  adaptive DB-LBT, red favors fixed TMC, and gray crosses zero.}
  \label{fig:generalization}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:generalization}(a) aggregates ten unseen topology, load, and
occupancy scenarios. Adaptive DB-LBT reaches $0.928023$, compared with
$0.926467$ for fixed TMC and $0.928067$ for the training-seed fixed oracle.
The paired gain is $0.001556$, with 95\% interval
$[0.001470,0.001651]$. Stable utility changes by only
$-7.0\times10^{-7}$, and Jain fairness stays within the registered
$-0.01$ tolerance.

Each confidence interval is formed from ten within-seed policy differences,
not from pooled rounds. The exogenous seed controls arrivals, occupancy, and
other scenario randomness shared by the compared policies. The fixed oracle is
chosen only from training seeds and is included as a reference for a single
non-adaptive tuple, not as an online upper bound.

The aggregate hides useful variation. Light Poisson traffic and the combined
dynamic case improve, heavy Poisson traffic slightly degrades, and several
periodic or sensing-perturbation cases tie at retained precision
[Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization}(b)]. Across the registered non-ideal set, the
gain is $0.289\%$, below the 10\% engineering target. The adaptive controller
is therefore most useful where local load and active-set information changes
the preferred recovery profile.

\subsection{Packet-Level Cross-Check}

The ns-3 model retains Wi-Fi ACK/EDCA behavior, NR-U HARQ and boundary
alignment, a 3GPP indoor frequency-selective channel, and a periodic waveform
interferer. Node positions and the sampled channel matrix are fixed. The
load-change case switches offered load halfway through its two-second run,
making it the closest packet-level realization of the intended phase change.

Across three paired seeds, adaptive-minus-fixed changes mix favorable and
unfavorable Wi-Fi/NR-U throughput and delay components. The event direction is
tied in the static and periodic cases; for the load change it is favorable,
whereas the packet-level majority is unfavorable. The stored ns-3 field named
\texttt{collision\_probability} is reported as packet-loss ratio because it is
computed from transmitted minus received IP packets and can include decoding
failure. The appendix gives every technology-level difference.

\subsection{What the Context Adds}

The combined dynamic event case isolates the controller choices. Replacing
LinUCB with context-free UCB lowers utility by $0.000686$, with 95\% interval
$[-0.001259,-0.000138]$. This is the clearest evidence that the local feature
vector matters. Removing queue, delay, or CCA/interruption features one group
at a time yields intervals that cross zero, as does freezing online updates.
The complete ablation forest is retained in the appendix.
